Go live on YouTube.
Straight from your Mac.

OnAir puts your camera, your screen and your Mac’s sound into one stream and sends it to YouTube. Pick a layout, rehearse, press Go live. No subscription, and none of the knobs.

Layouts

Four layouts.
One click each.

No scenes to build. Switch between them while you rehearse or while you are live, and the stream follows.

Camera

Just you, full frame. For the intro, the Q&A and the goodbye.

Screen

A whole display or a single window, full frame. Share one window and only that window shows.

Screen + camera

Your screen, with your camera in the bottom-right corner. The classic for demos and classes.

Side by side

Screen on the left, camera on the right, the same size. When you matter as much as the slides.

Rehearsal

Rehearse first.
Then go live.

Before anyone sees anything, OnAir shows you exactly what YouTube will get. Check the framing, the sound and the layouts, then go live from the same window.

  1. REHEARSAL

    Start rehearsal.

    Your camera, microphone and shared screen come up in a live preview. Nothing leaves your Mac yet.

  2. CONNECTING…

    Paste your key and go live.

    Copy the stream key from YouTube Studio once. OnAir keeps it in your Mac’s Keychain for next time.

  3. ● LIVE

    Switch layouts. End when you’re done.

    Change layouts as you talk. End stream stops the broadcast; Back to rehearsal gets you ready for the next one.

Sources

Everything a
stream needs.

Camera.

The Mac’s own camera, a webcam or your iPhone with Continuity Camera.

Microphone.

Any input your Mac knows: the built-in mic, AirPods or a USB microphone.

Screen or window.

Choose with macOS’s own picker. Share one window and your notifications and other apps stay private.

Your Mac’s sound.

The video or music you play goes into the stream, mixed with your voice. No extra audio drivers.

and OBS.

StreamYard is easy, runs in the browser and charges a subscription for the good parts. OBS is free and can do anything, if you learn it. OnAir keeps StreamYard’s simplicity and runs on your own Mac.

What it does not do, yet

YouTube only

One destination, over RTMPS with your stream key. Twitch and streaming to several platforms at once come later.

Just you

No remote guests, overlays or vertical video in this first version. YouTube keeps the recording of your stream.

Mac only

OnAir is built on macOS’s own capture and encoding, which is why it is light. It needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later.

Under the hood

Light, because
it’s native.

Capture, mixing and H.264 encoding happen on your Mac with Apple’s frameworks and the hardware encoder. No browser tab and no server in the middle.
